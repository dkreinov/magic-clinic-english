# Design — W5b, the word quiz (FROZEN)

Produced by a `grill-me` pass on 2026-07-26. Thirteen questions, all answered by the owner.
This is the WHAT. `plan.md` is the HOW. Nothing here is re-opened during execution.

## The ask (brief W5b, verbatim)

> **W5 — she must be able to say "I know this word", and then be TESTED on it.** […] once she claims
> a word, a re-exam — "interesting, similar to Duolingo or better, with images, sounds or whatever" —
> should check whether she really learned it, so she *feels* confident about what she knows.

W5a (the claim button) shipped 2026-07-26. This is the other half.

## Why this is not optional polish

`mark-known` writes `status: "known"`, `lib/vocab.js:knownLemmaSet` collects every such word, and
`lib/story.js:buildAllowedSet` feeds them into the story-generation prompt. **Her "I know it" button
is an input to content difficulty, not a trophy case.** A word she claims wrongly is then used
freely in every future chapter, WITHOUT a glossary entry (the glossary obligation covers only the
≤3 off-list words per chapter). Her reward gets harder and nothing tells her why.

W5a therefore shipped a one-way write into story generation with no correction path —
`docs/growth.md` G1 even states it: *"Promotion is one-way; nothing in this design ever demotes a
word back to `learning`."* **The quiz is the missing correction term, not a feature on top.**

## The fourteen locked decisions

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Failing a claimed word sends it back to `learning`, visibly, after **3 strikes** spread apart | Never-demote actively degrades her stories (see above). Visible + forgiving |
| D2 | She claims **honestly but optimistically** — wrong claims are mistakes, not gaming | Sets strikes at 3 |
| D3 | Item format is **cloze**: pick the missing word in a sentence | Kills "images for 2254 words"; emoji only ever worked for 12 hand-picked concrete nouns |
| D4 | **6 options**, same part of speech, a speaker button on each | 4→6 buys most of the anti-guess benefit (25%→17%); 6→8 costs real reading load for a child who cannot read the distractors |
| D5 | Distractors are **generated with the sentence**, pool of 8, 5 chosen at runtime preferring words she knows | A randomly drawn same-pos word sometimes genuinely fits the blank — marking her wrong for being right |
| D6 | **Static bank now**, chapter-harvested sentences layered on later | Works day one; free and better sentences arrive as she reads |
| D7 | Generated by **Claude via oplan workers**, cheap tier — no paid API, no runtime LLM, no second key | DeepSeek V4 Flash and Kimi K2.5/K2.6 were priced and set aside; at ~5k items the price difference is cents and the runtime dependency is the real cost |
| D8 | **All 2254 words**, ~5000 items | One-and-done; costs session time, not money |
| D9 | "Complicated" = **worker judgment**: one sentence per distinct meaning an 11-year-old would meet, 2 for abstract, 1 for concrete single-sense, **hard cap 3** | Polysemy is the real reason one sentence is not enough, and no mechanical rule can see it |
| D10 | Quiz runs **after each chapter** (4 words), **plus** a button inside `המילים שלי` — not a new nav tab | Only option that lands strikes on a predictable schedule without her opting in |
| D11 | Re-tapping a known word **flags it for priority quizzing, never strikes it** | W1 gave her a reason to tap words she knows — the speaker lives in that popup |
| D12 | `growth.md` G1 promotes to a **`candidate`** state; the quiz is what makes it trusted | Nothing the algorithm guessed reaches the generator unverified |
| D13 | **Asymmetric trust**: her claims enter the allowed set immediately (policed by strikes); G1 candidates must pass first | A child saying "I know this" is a stronger signal than "she did not tap it twice" |
| D14 | **Fully automatic now**, parent read-only view soon | The correction loop must never wait on a human |

## Correction — the learner is ELEVEN, not nine

Recorded 2026-07-26, before any code was written. I had "9-year-old" written into D9 and into
QZ-2 — a FROZEN contract that every one of the ~45 generation batches would have followed. It is
not cosmetic: it sets which senses of a polysemous word count as "one an 11-year-old would meet"
(an eleven-year-old meets the abstract senses — `fair` as just, `light` as not serious — that a
nine-year-old would not), and it sets the bar for age-appropriateness. All occurrences corrected
across design.md, plan.md, journal.md and STATUS.md.

**One decision it arguably reopens, left to the owner rather than changed unilaterally: D4's six
options.** My argument for 6 over 8 was partly "8 English distractors is heavy reading for a child
who taps words because she cannot read them". At eleven that argument is weaker, so 8 is now more
defensible than it was. The guessing arithmetic is unchanged: 6 options = 17% per attempt and 0.5%
surviving three strikes, 8 options = 12.5% and 0.2%. The owner chose 6 and 6 stands unless they say
otherwise.

## Decisions taken by the orchestrator at plan time (Rule 2: nothing deferred to execution)

The grill left five items "still open". They are decided here, because a step containing an open
question must never be dispatched.

- **D15 — a pass resets `strikes` to 0.** Otherwise a word she mostly knows accrues strikes over
  months and eventually demotes on a technicality. Three strikes means three failures with no
  success in between.
- **D16 — "spread apart" = at most one strike per word per quiz session.** Mechanical, needs no
  dates, and prevents three wrong taps in one sitting from wiping a word.
- **D17 — 4 words per after-chapter check.** Within the 3-5 the owner accepted; an even number
  reads better in a grid.
- **D18 — the third status is `candidate`.** `WORD_STATUSES` becomes
  `['known','learning','candidate']`. It is introduced in the phase that needs it (G1), not before.
- **D19 — early distractor pool.** When her known list is too small, options fall back to the item's
  own frozen 8 distractors. No special-casing, no empty quiz.

## What success looks like

She claims a word. Within a chapter or two she is asked about it in a sentence, among six words she
can read or hear. If she is right, nothing happens and she feels correct. If she is wrong three
separate times, the word quietly goes back to `learning` and the story starts teaching it again.

## Explicit non-goals

- No images, no emoji items, no new artwork.
- No runtime LLM call, ever, in this run.
- No paid API of any kind.
- No change to the six placement exam clips or `data/placement-items.json`.
- No spaced-repetition scheduling algorithm (SM-2 etc.). Ordering is: flagged, then recently
  claimed, then longest-unseen. That is all.
- No parent-facing view in this run (D14 says "soon", not now).

## The risk that outranks all others

**The mechanical gate can check the FORM of an item but not its MEANING.** Checkable: the sentence
uses only allowed words, exactly one blank, the answer is the lemma, distractors are in-vocabulary
and same-pos. NOT checkable: whether the sentence actually makes the meaning clear, and whether all
8 distractors are genuinely wrong in that sentence.

This run has already been bitten by exactly this shape twice — "valid ADTS header" passed 36 clips
containing no speech, and "filenames match the allowed set" passed a set that did not match the
words on screen. A bad item does not fail loudly: it marks her wrong for a right answer, in a
feature whose whole purpose is confidence.

**Therefore a human sample review is a phase gate, not a nicety.** The owner eyeballs a random 50
before the full bank is generated, and again before it ships.
